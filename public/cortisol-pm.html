What does your
Cortisol PM result mean?

Hormones
⚠️ Educational only. LabPlain does not provide medical advice or diagnosis. Always discuss your specific results with your healthcare provider.
What this test measures

Cortisol is a major hormone made by your adrenal glands. While it is best known for driving your "fight-or-flight" stress response, it also works constantly behind the scenes to control blood pressure, manage blood sugar, balance metabolism, and calm systemic inflammation.

A "Cortisol PM" test specifically checks your levels late in the afternoon or evening. Your brain and adrenal glands are designed to operate on a strict timer. Cortisol should crest dramatically right as you wake up to give you energy, and then steadily descend throughout the day to let your body rest and recover at night.

Doctors track late-day levels when they want to see if your internal clock is working correctly. If your evening cortisol fails to drop as expected, or if it bottoms out completely, it provides important clues about adrenal health, pituitary function, or conditions like Cushing's syndrome.

Normal reference range
Afternoon (4 PM): 2–11 mcg/dL  |  Late Evening (Midnight): Less than 5.0 mcg/dL

Cortisol relies on a daily wave pattern called a diurnal rhythm. Because your baseline level naturally decays over the course of the day, an afternoon or midnight baseline has a significantly lower normal cutoff point than a morning test.

Exact reference parameters depend entirely on the specific lab running the analysis and whether blood, saliva, or urine was used. Always reference the exact numbers provided on your final lab dashboard.

⏰ The Crucial PM Window

An afternoon draw is usually scheduled around 4 PM to capture the late-day dip. Midnight evaluations look for the lowest daily point. If your draw occurred outside these precise windows, the reference thresholds will shift significantly.

What your result might indicate
↑ If High

Elevated late-day levels can indicate that your diurnal rhythm is flattening. This pattern is often associated with high chronic emotional or physical stress, severe depression, sleep disorders, or early-stage Cushing's syndrome.

↓ If Low

While low evening values are generally healthy and expected, an exceptionally flat or bottomed-out afternoon reading may be seen in primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison's disease) or prolonged use of steroid medications.

Symptoms associated with abnormal cortisol

↑ High Late-Day Symptoms

  • "Wired but tired" feeling at bedtime
  • Inability to fall asleep or frequent waking
  • Weight retention around the face and stomach
  • Elevated evening blood pressure readings
  • Increased anxiety or racing thoughts at night
  • Slow healing from minor cuts or bruises

↓ Severe Low PM Symptoms

  • Profound, unrelenting fatigue throughout the day
  • Dizziness or lightheadedness when standing up
  • Unintended or unexpected weight loss
  • Distinct cravings for highly salty foods
  • Chronic muscle weakness or body aches
  • Frequent nausea or digestive discomfort
Common causes of abnormal cortisol

What can keep afternoon cortisol high?

The most frequent culprit behind a minor evening spike is persistent daily stress. When your mind or body remains on high alert from work pressures, poor sleep, or an injury, the brain continues sending signals to dump cortisol into your bloodstream when it should be winding down.

More pronounced, atypical elevations are often associated with Cushing's syndrome, a condition where a small benign tumor on the pituitary or adrenal glands causes continuous hormone overproduction. Long-term use of pharmaceutical steroids like prednisone will also mimic this presentation on clinical tests.

What can cause abnormally low levels?

Because cortisol is supposed to drop later in the day, a low PM value is usually a sign of a normal, healthy rhythm. However, if your afternoon or morning readings are both flatlined near zero, it typically points toward compromised adrenal output. This occurs in Addison's disease (where the adrenal cortex is damaged) or secondary adrenal insufficiency, where the pituitary gland fails to manufacture enough stimulating hormones.

Tests often ordered alongside cortisol pm

A single late-day level rarely gives a complete diagnostic picture. Your provider may order comparative screenings, such as:

  • Cortisol AM (8 AM Draw) — Checked side-by-side with your PM level to calculate the steepness of your daily drop.
  • ACTH (Adrenocorticotropic Hormone) — pinpoints whether the underlying issue stems from the brain's control center or the adrenal glands directly.
  • Late-Night Salivary Cortisol Test — An easy, at-home swab used to track the exact midnight low point without the stress of a needle draw.
  • 24-Hour Urinary Free Cortisol — Measures the aggregate volume of hormone filtered over an entire day rather than a brief single snapshot.
  • Dexamethasone Suppression Test — Uses a small dose of synthetic steroid overnight to evaluate if your hormonal feedback loop responds properly.
What to do next
An isolated out-of-range PM value is simply a single piece of a larger mosaic. Because your nervous system is deeply responsive to sleep architecture, evening workouts, travel across time zones, and transient mental stress, a single anomalous number should not cause panic. Your practitioner will evaluate these findings alongside your clinical history, physical symptoms, and matching morning lab trends.
Questions to ask your doctor
  • 01Does my late-day result suggest a normal diurnal drop, or is my daily curve flattening out?
  • 02Could my current sleep schedule, physical stress levels, or workout timings explain this afternoon variance?
  • 03Do you recommend a late-night salivary collection or a 24-hour collection to verify these findings?
  • 04Are any of my current inhalers, skin creams, or prescription pills interfering with these hormone readouts?
  • 05Based on my matching symptom presentation, is a consultation with an endocrinologist a logical next step?
Frequently asked questions
Why did my doctor order a PM test instead of an AM test?
Morning tests are great at checking if your body can produce enough cortisol overall. Afternoon and evening tests are ordered when your doctor suspects your body is producing too much hormone at night, or to map your natural daily rhythm.
Can a tough workout at lunchtime spike my afternoon cortisol?
Yes. Intense physical exercise acts as a temporary stressor that triggers an immediate spike in adrenal output. If you had a demanding workout a few hours before your blood draw, it may cause a temporarily higher PM reading.
What is the "wired but tired" phenomenon?
This is a common term for when your body is physically exhausted but your afternoon or evening cortisol levels remain abnormally elevated. It makes it incredibly difficult to wind down, fall asleep, or achieve deep sleep phases.
How does shift work affect a PM cortisol test?
Your diurnal cycle adapts directly to your sleep-wake habits. If you work night shifts or have irregular sleep cycles, your cortisol peak and drop times will flip, which means standard reference ranges will not match your true circadian health.
