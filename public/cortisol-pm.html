What does your
PM Cortisol result mean?

Hormones
⚠️ Educational only. LabPlain does not provide medical advice or diagnosis. A single lab result cannot diagnose a medical condition and should be interpreted alongside symptoms, medical history, medications, and other testing by a qualified healthcare professional.
What this test measures

A PM Cortisol test measures the amount of cortisol in your blood during the evening. Cortisol is often called the body's primary stress hormone and is produced by the adrenal glands. Because cortisol normally follows a daily rhythm, evening measurements can provide different information than morning cortisol testing.

Normal reference range
Often less than 10 mcg/dL in the evening*

*Reference ranges vary by laboratory, testing method, and collection time. Always use the reference interval shown on your own lab report when reviewing results.

What your result might indicate
↑ If High

Cortisol levels are normally much lower in the evening than in the morning. An elevated PM cortisol result may be associated with chronic stress, certain medications, depression, heavy alcohol use, sleep disruption, or conditions involving excess cortisol production. A single elevated result does not confirm any specific diagnosis and often requires additional testing.

↓ If Low

Lower evening cortisol levels are often part of the body's normal daily rhythm. Results that are unusually low may sometimes be seen with adrenal gland disorders, pituitary disorders, or after use of certain steroid medications. Interpretation depends heavily on the timing of the test and other clinical information.

Why timing matters

Cortisol follows a circadian rhythm. Levels are typically highest in the early morning and gradually fall throughout the day, reaching their lowest levels late in the evening. Because of this natural pattern, an evening cortisol result cannot be interpreted using the same reference range as a morning cortisol test.

Common symptoms associated with abnormal cortisol

Symptoms associated with cortisol abnormalities can vary widely and may overlap with many other medical conditions.

Higher cortisol levels may be associated with:

• Weight gain, especially around the abdomen
• High blood pressure
• Elevated blood sugar
• Sleep difficulties
• Mood changes or anxiety
• Easy bruising

Lower cortisol levels may be associated with:

• Fatigue
• Weakness
• Dizziness
• Unintentional weight loss
• Nausea
• Low blood pressure

These symptoms are not specific to cortisol disorders and can occur in many different health conditions.

What can affect the result?

Several factors can influence PM cortisol measurements, including recent stress, illness, sleep patterns, shift work, pregnancy, strenuous exercise, and medications such as corticosteroids, estrogen therapies, or certain seizure medications. Your healthcare provider may consider these factors when interpreting the result.

What to do next
PM cortisol is usually interpreted together with your symptoms, medical history, and sometimes additional testing such as morning cortisol, ACTH, salivary cortisol, dexamethasone suppression testing, or 24-hour urine cortisol testing. If your result falls outside the reference range, your healthcare provider can determine whether follow-up testing is appropriate.
