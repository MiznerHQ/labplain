What is a
DHEA-S Test?

Hormone Panel
⚠️ Educational reference only. LabPlain is an educational site, not a diagnostic tool. A single lab result must always be interpreted alongside clinical symptoms, medical history, and other evaluations by a qualified healthcare professional.
What this test measures

DHEA-S (Dehydroepiandrosterone Sulfate) is a blood test that measures the amount of an important precursor androgen hormone in your body. It is primarily made by your adrenal glands—the two small, triangular hats that sit on top of your kidneys—with much smaller amounts coming from the ovaries in females or testes in males.

Think of DHEA-S as a building block. Your body converts it into active male sex hormones (like testosterone) and active female sex hormones (like estrogen). Because it is almost exclusively produced by your adrenal glands, measuring its concentration is a helpful way to evaluate how well your adrenal factory is functioning.

Understanding the ranges
↑ Elevated Levels

Higher concentrations may indicate overactive adrenal glands. This is often seen in conditions like Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS), adrenal tumors, or congenital adrenal hyperplasia.

↓ Low Levels

Lower markers can be associated with underactive adrenal function (adrenal insufficiency), structural damage to the adrenal cortex, or low pituitary gland output.

⏰ Does timing matter?

Unlike regular DHEA or cortisol, which swing wildly up and down depending on the hour of the day, DHEA-S stays remarkably stable in your blood stream. You generally do not need to fast or time the test for a specific hour, though your doctor might want it coordinated with other hormone checks.

What the results can be associated with

Decoding your markers

Doctors look at DHEA-S alongside other sex hormones and adrenal compounds. Here is how physical trends track with the data:

  • Signs of higher levels in females — May reflect unexpected facial/body hair growth (hirsutism), severe acne, missing periods, or hair thinning.
  • Signs of higher levels in young children — Can be associated with early puberty markers or atypical physical development.
  • Signs of low levels overall — May mirror system-wide exhaustion, unexplained weight loss, low blood pressure, or low sex drive.
  • Age-related decline — Levels naturally peak in your 20s and steadily decrease as a normal part of the aging process.
Common accompanying lab panels

DHEA-S is rarely evaluated in isolation. Depending on what your practitioner is observing, it is regularly bundled with these tests:

  • Total & Free Testosterone — To look at the broader landscape of male sex hormone conversion.
  • Cortisol (Blood or Saliva) — To cross-reference overall adrenal output stability.
  • ACTH (Adrenocorticotropic Hormone) — To see if the brain is properly signaling the adrenals to perform.
  • 17-Hydroxyprogesterone — Frequently checked if genetic enzyme structural conditions are suspected.
Common interpretation mistakes
⚠️ DHEA vs. DHEA-S Confusion

They are different. DHEA-S has an attached sulfate group that makes it stay stable in your bloodstream for long periods. Standard DHEA leaves the body quickly and changes dynamically throughout the day.

⚠️ Looking at standalone metrics

An isolated high or low reading does not pinpoint a specific condition. Normal limits are highly sensitive to your age and biological sex.

What to do right now
If you have your lab panel results in front of you, match your age bracket and sex exactly against the specific reference range listed on your lab report sheet, as every testing company uses slightly different machines and calibrated thresholds. If your numbers look slightly off, do not panic—schedule a follow-up visit with your care team to crosscheck these metrics with physical symptoms.
Questions to ask your healthcare professional
  • 01Do my specific DHEA-S markers align perfectly with the normal baseline for my age and sex?
  • 02How do these results look when paired with my testosterone, cortisol, or thyroid panels?
  • 03Could my physical symptoms be explained by these numbers, or should we look elsewhere?
  • 04Are there any medications or supplements I am taking that could skew this blood metric?
  • 05Do we need to consider scheduling an ultrasound or imaging scan of my adrenal glands or ovaries?
Frequently asked questions
Can supplements change my DHEA-S levels?
Yes. Taking over-the-counter DHEA supplements or specific steroid medications can directly raise your blood concentration levels. Always tell your provider about everything you take.
What is the main difference between an ovarian issue and an adrenal issue?
Because the ovaries make very little DHEA-S but produce standard testosterone and estrogen, highly elevated DHEA-S typically points the diagnostic spotlight toward the adrenal glands rather than the ovaries.
Why do levels naturally go down with age?
It is a standard physiological progression. Adrenal production peaks during early adulthood and gradually falls by up to 80% over the course of a lifetime.
